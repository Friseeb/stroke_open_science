# Evidence: outsiders re-analysing shared evidence

For the opening of the WSC 2026 talk: cases where openness (a public dataset, a controlled-access platform, posted
results, released documents, recovered data) let people other than the original authors re-analyse the evidence.
Closed trialist pools (HERMES, Stroke Thrombolysis Trialists) are deliberately excluded. Found by three research agents
on 15 Oct 2026; each checked every PMID, DOI and number against PubMed (NCBI E-utilities) and, where open, the full
text. Status per item: VERIFIED, PARTLY VERIFIED (what is missing is named), NOT VERIFIED.

## Framing numbers (meta-research)

- Ebrahim 2014: 37 published reanalyses of RCT data (36 articles, MEDLINE to 9 Mar 2014). Only **5 by entirely
  independent authors** (2 from public data, 2 on request, 1 unclear). 13 (35%) changed who or how many should be
  treated; 4 changed direction, 4 changed significance. Ebrahim S et al. JAMA 2014;312:1024-32.
  doi:10.1001/jama.2014.9646. PMID 25203082. VERIFIED.
- Naudet 2018: 37 RCTs in BMJ and PLOS Medicine (full data-sharing policies, 2013-16). Data obtained for 17 (46%);
  **14 of 17 fully reproduced**; errors in 2, conclusions similar. Naudet F et al. BMJ 2018;360:k400.
  doi:10.1136/bmj.k400. PMID 29440066. VERIFIED.
- Reading: independent reversals are rare, partly because access is rare; where data are shared, most results
  reproduce. The case for openness is verification with occasional correction.

## Stroke

### NINDS rt-PA trial (the archetype)
- Data released October 2003, about 8 years after the 1995 NEJM paper, sold to the public on CD-ROM for US$79 (624
  patients, ~100 variables, SAS, no codebook). Dachs RJ et al. PLoS Med 2008;5:e113. doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.0050113.
  PMID 18494557. VERIFIED. Earlier refusal to outside critics, including a FOIA request: PARTLY VERIFIED (Dachs cites
  Lenzer, BMJ 2002;324:723, not opened).
- Original: global OR 1.7 (1.2-2.6); sICH 6.4% vs 0.6%. NEJM 1995;333:1581-7. PMID 7477192.
- Baseline imbalance: NIHSS 0-5 in 42 tPA vs 16 placebo patients (MHRA document).
- Independent committee (set up May 2002 at NINDS's request; chair O'Fallon, Mayo, not part of earlier t-PA work;
  3 statisticians, 3 clinicians; full data through an independent contractor): adjusted OR **2.1 (1.5-2.9)**, so the
  imbalance did not explain the benefit. New findings: the data "failed to support" a diminishing effect with
  onset-to-treatment time within 3 h (likely low power; pooled data later showed a gradient); blood-pressure data "too
  unreliable for inclusion in analysis". Ingall TJ et al. Stroke 2004;35:2418-24. doi:10.1161/01.STR.0000140891.70547.56.
  PMID 15345796. Hertzberg V et al. Clin Trials 2008;5:308-15. PMID 18697845. VERIFIED.
- BP consequence: Lindley RI, Wardlaw JM, Sandercock PA. Lancet Neurol 2005;4:249-53. PMID 15778104 (BP-control
  recommendations derived from NINDS "need to be reconsidered"). VERIFIED.
- Outside users of the public file: Hoffman JR, Schriger DL. Ann Emerg Med 2009;54:329-36. PMID 19464756 (advantage
  "diminish[es] or disappear[s]" on 90-day NIHSS change; how they got the data not confirmed). Mandava P et al.
  Stroke 2010;41:765-70. PMID 20167911 (matched reanalysis: benefit persisted, 13-23% smaller). Saver JL et al. Stroke
  2010;41:2381-90. PMID 20829518 (defends benefit). Garg R, Mickenautsch S. BMC Med Res Methodol 2022;22:172.
  PMID 35705913; reply Lyden P et al. 2023;23:275. PMID 37990152. VERIFIED.
- Regulator: UK Commission on Human Medicines expert working group (2015): the change-from-baseline analysis is
  "biased against the group with more mild strokes"; the committee's methods handle the imbalance better; "no
  regulatory implications". assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/.../448997/Final_conclusions_and_recommendations.pdf.
  VERIFIED.
- 2026 coda: the NINDS Archived Clinical Research Datasets page (checked 15 Oct 2026): "This repository is under review
  for potential modification in compliance with Administration directives" and "data requests will no longer be
  accepted. If you require access to these datasets, please contact the Principal Investigators (PIs) of the respective
  studies. We expect the migration to be completed by the end of 2026." VERIFIED (page fetched).

#### NINDS rt-PA, further detail (checked directly on 15 Oct 2026)
- Trial design: part 1 (291 patients; primary outcome, NIHSS improvement at 24 h, not significant) and part 2 (333
  patients; global OR 1.7, 1.2-2.6). Mortality at 3 months 17% vs 21% (P = 0.30). PubMed abstract, PMID 7477192.
- Dachs 2008 (full text, PMC2386834) lists the criticisms: no early improvement; enrolment split 0-90 / 91-180 min;
  baseline imbalance favouring rt-PA; small sample; inconsistency with earlier thrombolysis trials. "Access to this data
  was initially denied, even after petition to the Food and Drug Administration under the Freedom of Information Act,
  heightening concern and suspicions regarding the trial findings" (citing Lenzer, BMJ 2002;324:723). "During this
  period, the NINDS authors published no less than 27 reports using various permutations of the NINDS dataset." By
  2008 only three reports by non-NINDS researchers had used the released data. NTIS CD-ROM, US$79 plus US$5 shipping;
  multiple SAS conversions; "no key accompanies the dataset".
- The review followed BMJ reporting: Lenzer J. BMJ 2002;324:723-9 (PMID 11909792); "Controversial stroke trial is under
  review following BMJ report", BMJ 2002;325:1131 (PMID 12433750). Full texts not readable here (scanned / cookie wall),
  so their claims (e.g. on Genentech funding) are NOT VERIFIED and stay off the slides.
- Committee terms of reference (Ingall abstract): "to address whether there is concern that eligible stroke patients may
  not benefit from t-PA given according to the protocol used in the trials and, whether the subgroup imbalance (in
  baseline stroke severity) invalidates the entire trial." Adjusted for centre, time to treatment, study part, age,
  baseline NIHSS, diabetes and pre-existing disability (MHRA document).
- Hertzberg 2008 (abstract): "With new NIH requirements for data-sharing, the frequency of re-analysis of clinical trial
  data may increase substantially. This re-evaluation provides a blueprint for future re-evaluations ... Primary
  investigators should address significant errors determined in such re-analyses."
- Lindley, Wardlaw, Sandercock 2005 (abstract): the reanalysis "established that this [imbalance] did not materially
  affect the positive results"; BP recommendations from NINDS "needs to be reconsidered"; old trials "probably
  exhausted"; new trials needed.
- Saver JL, Gornbein J, Starkman S 2010 (abstract): "Using the publicly available datasets"; tPA patients recovered a
  median 66.7% of the achievable change vs 50.0% with placebo (P = 0.003); criticises Hoffman & Schriger's axis scaling.
- Mandava 2010 (abstract): benefit "apparent mostly in the large vessel occlusion subtype".
- Hoffman & Schriger 2009 (abstract): also "Our graphs fail to support the time-is-brain hypothesis"; five published
  comments in Ann Emerg Med 2009-2010.
- MHRA/CHM Expert Working Group 2015 (full text): set up after the CHM considered "specific concerns that had been
  communicated to the MHRA" in May 2014. On NINDS: randomisation errors had "no impact" per an FDA analysis; "50% of
  patients enrolled in the 0-90 minute time window were treated between 89-90 minutes", so the NINDS data "are
  insufficient" to test time-is-brain; NIBSC found placebo and rt-PA vials foamed alike; Hoffman & Schriger's plots by
  baseline severity "are consistent with the findings of the NINDS re-analysis committee"; with NINDS removed from the
  meta-analysis "the results were qualitatively the same, although less robust". BMJ news: Cohen D. BMJ
  2014;349:g5355 (PMID 25179758), review announced; Brown SGA, Macdonald SPJ, Hankey GJ. BMJ 2013;347:f5215
  (PMID 23990634).
- Garg & Mickenautsch 2022 (full text): used the participant-level data plus the FDA product licensing application;
  11 of 16 randomisation strata deviated from 1:1; 3 centres broke an a-priori allocation rule; after adjustment e.g.
  mRS (0-90 min) aOR 1.6 (1.0-2.6), P = 0.059; all adjusted ORs still favour rt-PA; the global OR was not recomputed.
- Lyden et al. 2023 (eight original investigators, full text): concealment vs blinding; 13 wrong-vial errors (11 got
  placebo instead of rt-PA), 1 packaging error, 18 wrong-stratum vials (10 placebo instead of rt-PA, 1 the reverse), all
  disclosed to FDA; FDA reviewer: "this error in the randomization process appears not to have contributed any bias to
  overestimating the treatment effect"; "we write now to flatly deny that somehow treatment assignment was
  manipulated". Data statement: "All data from the original trial is available from the NINDS."

### ECASS III re-analysed through ClinicalStudyDataRequest.com (contested)
- Alper, Foster, Thabane, Rae-Grant, Malone-Moses, Manheimer (EBSCO/DynaMed, McMaster) obtained Boehringer Ingelheim
  IPD through CSDR (proposal 1619), remote SAS analysis; access "took some years".
- Analyses used a modified NIHSS (0-46) while the paper stated 0-42; with 0-42 the secondary outcomes lost significance
  (global OR 1.27, 0.995-1.63). The published adjusted OR 1.42 (1.02-1.98) reproduced only under three choices not in
  the protocol; seven alternative specifications gave P = 0.054-0.173. Adjusted for NIHSS and prior stroke, no efficacy
  outcome significant (mRS 0-1 RR 1.05, 0.95-1.16); sICH increase persisted. Authors: reanalysis "cannot confidently
  produce new conclusions" but "greatly reduces the certainty".
- Alper BS et al. BMJ Evid Based Med 2020;25:168-71. doi:10.1136/bmjebm-2020-111386. PMID 32430395. Original: Hacke W
  et al. NEJM 2008;359:1317-29. PMID 18815396. VERIFIED.
- Caveats: authors had publicly called for independent analysis before (BMJ 2015;350:h1075); guidelines unchanged (ESO
  2021 still rates evidence high within 4.5 h); Emberson 2014 IPD meta-analysis (Lancet, PMID 25106063) supports
  benefit to 4.5 h.

### Other stroke items
- IST-1 open data (19,435 patients, Edinburgh DataShare, 2011; accessed 10,131 times by Nov 2017); IST-3 IPD open since
  25 Jan 2021. Outside reuse mostly methodological (e.g. Nguyen 2017 J Clin Epidemiol, PMID 28257927: chance imbalance
  persists below ~1,000 participants). No treatment conclusion changed. Sandercock PA et al. Trials 2011;12:101.
  PMID 21510853. VERIFIED. "Open data done right", not "overturned".
- VISTA forbids treatment-effect re-analysis by design: "VISTA does not support re-analysis of specific trials or use of
  trial datasets for meta-analysis of treatment effects originally studied by the trial"; contributors are promised
  "there is no risk of re-analysis of any data used for a regulatory submission, publication or patent application";
  allocation masked; steering committee approves; subscription fee. VERIFIED (website).
- Sato fabrication: outside statisticians tested the baseline tables of 33 RCTs by one group (many in hemiplegic
  stroke): P = 5.2e-82; hip-fracture RR 0.22 in the trials. About 30 stroke-related retractions (JAMA 2005, Arch
  Intern Med 2005, Neurology 2003, several in Stroke). 12 retracted reports cited in 1,158 publications; 13 guidelines or
  reviews would likely change. Bolland MJ et al. Neurology 2016;87:2391-402. PMID 27920281; Avenell A et al. BMJ Open
  2019;9:e031909. PMID 31666272. VERIFIED. Forensics of published summaries, not shared data.
- Cerebrolysin, the closed-data case: Cochrane (independent) could not obtain unpublished manufacturer data;
  2023 update: no effect on death, RR 0.96 (0.65-1.41); non-fatal SAEs RR 2.39 (1.10-5.23) (PMID 37818733). An
  industry-linked meta-analysis with original data for 5 of 9 trials concluded benefit (Bornstein 2018, PMID 29248999).
  VERIFIED.
- NXY-059 / CAMARADES: published animal data, not shared data (Macleod 2008, PMID 18635842; Sena 2010, PMID 20361022).
- No outsider reanalysis of IMS III, SAMMPRIS, SPS3, POINT, CLEAR III, MISTIE III, ARUBA, CREST, DEFUSE 3 etc. that
  changed a conclusion was found (negative search, not proof).

## Neuroscience and neurology

- **Cluster failure.** Resting-state scans of 499 healthy controls from the 1000 Functional Connectomes Project (Beijing
  198, Cambridge 198, Oulu 103) used as null data; 3 million random group analyses; cluster inference in SPM, FSL and
  AFNI gave familywise error "up to 70%" against a nominal 5%; a 15-year-old AFNI 3dClustSim bug found. "This study
  would not be possible without the recent data-sharing initiatives." The "40,000 studies" sentence was corrected
  (PNAS 2016;113:E4929, PMID 27503879). Eklund A, Nichols TE, Knutsson H. PNAS 2016;113:7900-5.
  doi:10.1073/pnas.1602413113. PMID 27357684. VERIFIED. Caveat: AFNI (Cox 2017, PMID 28398812) says 70% is the worst
  case; the paper questions methods, not specific studies.
- **Lesion network mapping.** van den Heuvel MP et al. Nat Neurosci 2026;29:1237-47. doi:10.1038/s41593-025-02196-7.
  PMID 41540261. "All data used in the present study are publicly available." Replies: Zalesky & Cash 2026 (PMID
  41912664); Siddiqi, Fox et al. bioRxiv 2026 (1,090 lesions, 34 studies, specificity holds); Pini, Salvalaggio,
  Corbetta, Nat Neurosci 2026;29:2051-3 (PMID 42162275), limits of LNM for diaschisis. Present as a live debate.
  VERIFIED.
- **Gabapentin** (litigation documents released 2008): of 12 published trials with internal documents, 8 reported a
  primary outcome different from the protocol; of 21 protocol primary outcomes, 6 unreported and 4 demoted. Vedula SS
  et al. NEJM 2009;361:1963-71. PMID 19907043. VERIFIED. Caveat: Dickersin was a plaintiffs' expert witness.
- **ALS PRO-ACT / DREAM challenge:** outside data scientists' models beat clinicians at predicting progression; could cut
  trial size by at least 20% (modelled). Küffner R et al. Nat Biotechnol 2015;33:51-7. PMID 25362243. VERIFIED.
- **NARPS:** 70 teams, one open fMRI dataset, nine hypotheses; no two pipelines alike. Nature 2020;582:84-8.
  PMID 32483374. VERIFIED.
- **Marek 2022** (ABCD, HCP, UK Biobank; ~50,000): median |r| 0.01; debated (Spisak 2023 and reply). VERIFIED.
- Lesné Aβ*56: image forensics of published figures, retracted 2024 (PMID 38914864); not an open-data case.

## Cardiology

- **Rosiglitazone.** Nissen & Wolski used results GSK had to post online under the 26 Aug 2004 New York Attorney
  General settlement over paediatric paroxetine data: 42 trials, MI OR 1.43 (1.03-1.98). NEJM 2007;356:2457-71.
  PMID 17517853. Followed by EMA suspension and FDA restriction (23 Sep 2010); FDA lifted restrictions 25 Nov 2013
  after the RECORD readjudication (Mahaffey 2013, PMID 23895806), noting "some scientific uncertainty". Wallach JD et al.
  (Yale) obtained IPD for 33 trials (21,156 patients) through CSDR: in **26 of 29** trials the IPD showed more MIs
  than the summaries earlier meta-analyses used; composite OR 1.33 (1.09-1.61). BMJ 2020;368:l7078. PMID 32024657;
  correction 2021 (PMID 34078655). VERIFIED. Caveat: Diamond, Bax & Kaul 2007 (PMID 17679700), method-dependent.
- **Minnesota Coronary Experiment / Sydney Diet Heart Study** (recovered 1960s-70s data; insiders' relatives and
  colleagues helped): MCE (n = 9,423) cholesterol fell 13.8% vs 1.0% with no mortality benefit; SDHS all-cause death
  HR 1.62 (1.00-2.64). Ramsden CE et al. BMJ 2016;353:i1246 (PMID 27071971); BMJ 2013;346:e8707 (PMID 23386268).
  VERIFIED.
- **SPRINT Data Analysis Challenge** (NEJM, 2016-17; 143 entries): outsiders (Yale) found the published Framingham
  risk profile wrong (≥15% risk in 76%, not 61%); NEJM corrected the paper (PMID 29262284). Warner F et al. BMJ Open
  2018;8:e021685. PMID 30037874. VERIFIED. Main conclusion unchanged.
- **Surgisphere** (the closed-data case): Lancet hydroxychloroquine paper (online 22 May 2020) and NEJM cardiovascular
  paper (online 1 May 2020) retracted 4-5 June 2020 because the data could not be given to an independent auditor;
  the Lancet then required data-sharing statements. Retractions PMID 32511943 and 32501665; "Learning from a
  retraction", Lancet 2020;396:1056 (PMID 32950071). VERIFIED.
- TOPCAT: regional anomaly found by insiders; outsiders (Berkowitz 2019, BioLINCC) confirmed methodologically. Do not
  present as an outsider reversal.

## Classics

- **Study 329** (paroxetine, adolescents): RIAT re-analysis by an external team using GSK's posted CSR, remote IPD and
  ~77,000 pages of case report forms: no efficacy on any prespecified outcome; suicidal or self-injurious behaviour
  11 / 4 / 2 (paroxetine / imipramine / placebo) vs 5 / 3 / 1 published. Le Noury J et al. BMJ 2015;351:h4320.
  PMID 26376805. Original Keller 2001 not retracted; expression of concern 2026 (PMID 41925712). VERIFIED. The same
  2004 New York settlement produced the GSK register that Nissen used.
- **Tamiflu**: Cochrane obtained clinical study reports (83 trials, ~150,000 pages) from EMA and Roche after a
  four-year campaign: symptoms 16.8 h shorter, no effect on hospital admission. Jefferson T et al. BMJ 2014;348:g2545.
  PMID 24811411. VERIFIED. Counter-analysis: Dobson 2015 Lancet (Roche-funded IPD, PMID 25640810).
- **Reinhart-Rogoff**: a graduate student given the working spreadsheet found the >90% debt growth figure was 2.2%, not
  -0.1%. Herndon T, Ash M, Pollin R. Camb J Econ 2014;38:257-79. VERIFIED.
- **Ariely**: data posted with a 2020 replication exposed fabrication; PNAS 2012 retracted 2021 (PMID 34518237).
  VERIFIED.
- **Ivermectin / Elgazzar**: raw data examined by outsiders; preprint withdrawn July 2021; the meta-analysis benefit
  disappeared without it. Lawrence JM et al. Nat Med 2021;27:1853-4. PMID 34552263. VERIFIED.
