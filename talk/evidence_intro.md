# Evidence for the opening slides (verified 2026-10-13)

Sources for slide 01 ("In lesion neuroscience, the whole pipeline is open"), slide 02 ("The fields next door had
their crisis first") and slide 03 ("The rules now exist. Practice is catching up."). Every claim below was found by
one research agent and then re-checked against its source by an independent agent that opened the DOI page, PubMed,
Europe PMC, the journal or the official repository. "corrected" means the checker changed a number, year or wording;
the corrected version is the one given. Nothing was refuted. Counts that change over time (OpenNeuro datasets,
NeuroVault images, BIDS version) are as read on 2026-10-13.

Caveats worth knowing before questions:

- Several lesion tools need MATLAB (NiiStat, the SVR-LSM GUI, the Lesion Quantification Toolkit, the SPM Clinical
  Toolbox). ATLAS raw data, the Johns Hopkins set and the GSP1000 connectome sit behind data use agreements.
  ENIGMA Stroke Recovery shares code, not data.
- BIDS has a lesion-mask convention in BIDS Derivatives (label "L" = lesion), although fMRIPrep's older docs say
  lesion masks are not part of BIDS.
- Protzko et al. 2023 (86% replication with rigour-enhancing practices) was retracted on 24 September 2024; do not
  cite it as evidence. The p < 0.005 proposal (Benjamin 2018) was contested ("Justify your alpha", 88 authors).
- Lesion network mapping is under live debate: van den Heuvel et al., Nat Neurosci 2026, and replies in the same
  journal and on bioRxiv. Present it as a debate made possible by open data, not as settled.
- Marek 2022 ("thousands of participants") is debated (Spisak 2023 and the reply); present it as debated.

## Open-source lesion analysis: tools, datasets and standards (slide 01, "Where I come from")

- **L01** (corrected). MRIcron (Rorden, Karnath & Bonilha 2007) is a free viewer for drawing lesions. It implements the Liebermeister test for binary data and the Brunner-Munzel test for continuous data for voxel-based lesion mapping. It succeeds MRIcro (Rorden & Brett 2000) and is BSD-licensed (license.txt in neurolabusc/MRIcron). Its successor MRIcroGL (rordenlab/MRIcroGL) uses a BSD-style 3-clause licence. Rorden C, Karnath HO, Bonilha L. Improving lesion-symptom mapping. J Cogn Neurosci 2007;19:1081-1088. Rorden C, Brett M. Stereotaxic display of brain lesions. Behav Neurol 2000;12:191-200 (doi 10.1155/2000/421719). doi:10.1162/jocn.2007.19.7.1081 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1162/jocn.2007.19.7.1081%22&resultType=core&format=json>
- **L02** (confirmed). ITK-SNAP (Yushkevich 2006) is an open-source tool for semi-automatic segmentation using active contours (level sets). It is GPL-3.0 on GitHub. ATLAS v2.0 lesions were traced in ITK-SNAP 3.8.0 instead of MRIcron because of its semi-automated lesion interpolation. Yushkevich PA et al. NeuroImage 2006;31:1116-1128. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2006.01.015 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9203460/fullTextXML>
- **L03** (confirmed). LINDA (Pustina 2016) segments chronic stroke lesions automatically from a single T1-weighted MRI. Against manual tracing in 60 left-hemisphere chronic stroke patients its mean Dice was 0.696 ± 0.16, and it was tested on an independent set of 45 patients. It ships with models trained on more than 100 patients and is Apache-2.0 on GitHub. Pustina D et al. Hum Brain Mapp 2016;37:1405-1421. doi:10.1002/hbm.23110 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1002/hbm.23110%22&resultType=core&format=json>
- **L04** (corrected). nnU-Net (Isensee 2021) is a self-configuring deep-learning segmentation framework. It surpassed most existing approaches, including highly specialised ones, on 23 public challenge datasets, and it is Apache-2.0 on GitHub. In ISLES'22, nnU-Net and U-Net-like networks were the most prevalent architectures, and the winning team used nnU-Net. Isensee F et al. Nat Methods 2021;18:203-211. doi:10.1038/s41592-020-01008-z <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12335569/fullTextXML>
- **L05** (confirmed). DeepISLES (de la Rosa 2025) is an ensemble built from the top ISLES'22 submissions. On an external cohort of 1,685 patients it outperformed the previous state of the art by 7.4% in Dice and 12.6% in F1. It is released as source code, a Docker image, a web service and a GUI under Apache-2.0. de la Rosa E et al. Nat Commun 2025;16:7357. doi:10.1038/s41467-025-62373-x <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12335569/fullTextXML>
- **L06** (confirmed). In ISLES'22 (May-August 2022), 476 participants registered and the dataset was downloaded 325 times. Twenty teams passed the sanity-check phase and 15 submitted deep-learning solutions as Docker containers in the final test phase, of which 12 met the participation criteria. The top three used nnU-Net, SegResNet and a non-negative matrix factorisation approach. de la Rosa E et al. Nat Commun 2025;16:7357. doi:10.1038/s41467-025-62373-x <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12335569/fullTextXML>
- **L07** (confirmed). Cost-function masking (Brett 2001) leaves the lesion out of the normalisation cost function so that it does not bias the warp. The authors proposed it as routine for brains with focal lesions. Brett M, Leff AP, Rorden C, Ashburner J. NeuroImage 2001;14:486-500. doi:10.1006/nimg.2001.0845 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1006/nimg.2001.0845%22&resultType=core&format=json>
- **L08** (confirmed). Enantiomorphic normalisation (Nachev 2008) replaces the signal in the lesion with the homologous undamaged region of the other hemisphere before non-linear warping. In the authors' simulations, with artificial lesions on normal brains, it was "vastly superior" to cost-function masking. The paper is CC BY. Nachev P et al. NeuroImage 2008;39:1215-1226. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.10.002 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.10.002%22&resultType=core&format=json>
- **L09** (confirmed). The SPM Clinical Toolbox (Rorden 2012) provides CT and MRI templates from healthy adults of stroke age (mean 65 years), with lesion masking, for normalising clinical scans. The toolbox is BSD-2-Clause on GitHub and SPM itself is GPL-2.0. Rorden C, Bonilha L, Fridriksson J, Bender B, Karnath HO. NeuroImage 2012;61:957-965. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.03.020 <https://api.github.com/repos/neurolabusc/Clinical>
- **L10** (confirmed). ANTs, with its SyN symmetric diffeomorphic registration (Avants 2008), is an open-source registration library that is Apache-2.0 on GitHub. The 2011 ANTs evaluation paper released its open code, data and evaluation scripts. Avants BB et al. Med Image Anal 2008;12:26-41; Avants BB et al. NeuroImage 2011;54:2033-2044. doi:10.1016/j.media.2007.06.004 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1016/j.neuroimage.2010.09.025%22&resultType=core&format=json>
- **L11** (corrected). fMRIPrep (Esteban 2019; Apache-2.0) accepts a binary lesion mask in anat/ (sub-XX_label-lesion_roi.nii.gz), which ANTs uses for cost-function masking during normalisation to standard space. The docs say lesion masks are "not currently part of the BIDS specification", but the same section notes that these instructions predate BIDS Derivatives. Since BIDS 1.4.0 the recommended form is a derivative mask (e.g. manual_masks/sub-001/anat/sub-001_desc-tumor_mask.nii.gz), and fMRIPrep plans to read lesion masks from derivatives. Esteban O et al. Nat Methods 2019;16:111-116; fMRIPrep documentation (stable), 'Cost function masking during spatial normalization'. doi:10.1038/s41592-018-0235-4 <https://fmriprep.org/en/stable/workflows.html>
- **L12** (confirmed). Bates et al. introduced voxel-based lesion-symptom mapping (VLSM) in Nature Neuroscience in 2003. Bates E et al. Nat Neurosci 2003;6:448-450. doi:10.1038/nn1050 <https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esummary.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=12704393&retmode=json>
- **L13** (confirmed). NiiStat (Rorden lab) is a GPL-2.0 set of MATLAB scripts for analysing neuroimaging data from clinical populations, including voxelwise, region-of-interest and connectome (DTI/resting-state) analyses with a lesion modality. It is distributed through GitHub and NITRC. NiiStat, github.com/neurolabusc/NiiStat (GPL-2.0); NITRC project niistat <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/neurolabusc/NiiStat/master/README.md>
- **L14** (confirmed). LESYMAP (Pustina 2018) is an R package for multivariate lesion-symptom mapping using sparse canonical correlation analysis (SCCAN). In simulations based on 131 chronic stroke patients, SCCAN was more accurate than mass-univariate VLSM. The package is Apache-2.0, has a Docker image and is archived on Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.1452007). Pustina D et al. Neuropsychologia 2018;115:154-166. doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2017.08.027 <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dorianps/LESYMAP/master/README.md>
- **L15** (confirmed). Support-vector-regression lesion-symptom mapping (SVR-LSM; Zhang 2014) models the relation between the symptom and the whole lesion map at once. On synthetic data it had much higher sensitivity and specificity than VLSM. Zhang Y, Kimberg DY, Coslett HB, Schwartz MF, Wang Z. Hum Brain Mapp 2014;35:5861-5876. doi:10.1002/hbm.22590 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1002/hbm.22590%22&resultType=core&format=json>
- **L16** (confirmed). DeMarco & Turkeltaub (2018) released a graphical SVR-LSM toolbox (GPL-3.0) with permutation-based cluster-level FWE correction and several lesion-volume corrections. They showed a strong bias toward brain-wide lesion-deficit associations, in both SVR-LSM and VLSM, when lesion volume is not adequately controlled. DeMarco AT, Turkeltaub PE. Hum Brain Mapp 2018;39:4169-4182. doi:10.1002/hbm.24289 <https://api.github.com/repos/atdemarco/svrlsmgui>
- **L17** (confirmed). BCBtoolkit (Foulon 2018, GigaScience, CC BY) estimates the direct and indirect disconnections caused by a lesion and was applied to 37 patients with focal frontal lesions. It is freely available at toolkit.bcblab.com, and its scripts are BSD-3-Clause on GitHub. Foulon C et al. GigaScience 2018;7(3):1-17. doi:10.1093/gigascience/giy004 <https://api.github.com/repos/chrisfoulon/BCBToolKit>
- **L18** (confirmed). The Lesion Quantification Toolkit (Griffis 2021) is a publicly available MATLAB package. It estimates parcel-level grey-matter damage and disconnection for 70 white-matter tracts of the HCP-842 tractography atlas (N = 842 healthy HCP participants). It requires the free DSI Studio and is downloaded from a WUSTL Box link. Griffis JC, Metcalf NV, Corbetta M, Shulman GL. NeuroImage Clin 2021;30:102639. doi:10.1016/j.nicl.2021.102639 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8053805/fullTextXML>
- **L19** (confirmed). Lesion network mapping (Boes 2015) transfers each lesion to a reference brain and computes its functional connectivity in normative connectome data to find a network that lesions share. In peduncular hallucinosis, 22 of 23 heterogeneous lesions were negatively correlated with extrastriate visual cortex. The method was then tested in three more syndromes. Boes AD et al. Brain 2015;138:3061-3075. doi:10.1093/brain/awv228 <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1093/brain/awv228%22&resultType=core&format=json>
- **L20** (corrected). The GSP1000 Preprocessed Connectome (Cohen, Soussand, McManus, Fox; Harvard Dataverse doi:10.7910/DVN/ILXIKS, version 3.0) is a widely used normative connectome for lesion network mapping. It contains 1,000 of the 1,570 Brain Genomics Superstruct Project participants and is released only under the original GSP terms of use. A version ready for Lead-DBS is at doi:10.7910/DVN/KKTJQC. Cohen A, Soussand L, McManus P, Fox M. GSP1000 Preprocessed Connectome, Harvard Dataverse, V3 (2021); Holmes AJ et al. Sci Data 2015;2:150031. doi:10.7910/DVN/ILXIKS <https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/datasets/:persistentId/?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/ILXIKS>
- **L21** (corrected). In 2026, a Nature Neuroscience re-analysis by van den Heuvel et al. (online 15 Jan 2026) used only public data (GSP1000, NeuroVault maps, Lead-DBS, LesionBank). Its survey found 201 LNM studies covering 101 conditions (2015-2025), and re-analysing 102 published LNM networks from 72 studies showed high overall alignment. Two Comment pieces followed in Nat Neurosci. Zalesky & Cash (online 30 Mar 2026) proposed robust null models but argued the convergence is clinically informative. Pini, Salvalaggio & Corbetta (online 20 May 2026) cited independent clinical evidence that supports the concerns. van den Heuvel MP et al. ## Psychology and neuropsychology: the crisis, the consensus, where the field is now (slides 02 and 03)

- **R01** (confirmed). Ioannidis's 2005 essay used modelling to argue that, for most study designs and settings, a published research claim is more likely false than true. Small studies, small effects, flexible designs and analyses, and many teams chasing statistical significance all make a claim less likely to be true. Ioannidis JPA. Why most published research findings are false. PLoS Med 2005;2(8):e124. doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124 <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16060722/>
- **R02** (confirmed). Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn (2011) simulated 15,000 samples per scenario. Using four common analysis choices together raised the false-positive rate at p < .05 from the nominal 5% to 60.7%.
