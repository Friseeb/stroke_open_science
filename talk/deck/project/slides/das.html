16,362 open-access stroke full texts, up to 1,500 a year · Gabelica 2022

Sample: 16,362 open-access stroke full texts, up to 1,500 per year, Europe PMC; optimistic for stroke overall. Own deposit 5.9% in 2025. Statements arrived with mandates. Elsewhere, "on request" delivers little: 6.8% of authors shared when asked (Gabelica 2022); 20% of 391 vascular and cognitive trials, median 241 days (Scutt 2020). The slide people photograph. Three numbers, slowly. Against the other fields, 2022 to 2025: stroke says “on request” more than any other field (30%; neurology 27%, neuroscience 26%, cardiology 24%), writes statements as often as any of them (69%; neuroscience 72%, neurology 64%, cardiology 61%), and deposits or names a public dataset at the level of the other clinical fields (6.9%; cardiology 7.3%, neurology 5.6%), well under neuroscience (17.5%). Neuroscience caveat: its title term “neural” also catches machine-learning papers; without them neuroscience writes statements in 68% (stroke 69%) and deposits 13.6%. If asked for the decade: “on request” 19% and deposit or public source 4.2%, pooled over yearly samples of up to 1,500, so every year weighs the same; they are not corpus rates. Full text versus abstract for the same audited articles, 2015 to 2025 pooled the same way: GitHub in 1.3% of full texts vs code, OSF or Zenodo links in 0.07% of abstracts; trial registration 14.1% vs 7.5%. If asked about top journals: 0.6% of top-journal RCTs that declared sharing had downloadable data (Danchev 2021).
