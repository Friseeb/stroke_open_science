An incentives agenda for stroke: six items, each with an owner

1

Journals · costs nothing

Retire "available on request"

A repository, a controlled-access platform such as VISTA or Vivli, or a stated reason. Verified at acceptance, not self-declared.

2

Journals and WSO · needs funding

Registered Reports for stroke

In-principle acceptance tied to WSO and ESO grant awards, as the Children's Tumor Foundation did with PLOS ONE. Nature opened the format to clinical science in June 2026.

3

WSO · costs nothing

A position statement and a reuse prize

No stroke society has one. A WSO award for reuse of stroke data, presented at this congress, on the Einstein Foundation model.

4

Funders · needs funding

Count datasets and code as outputs

CIHR's narrative CV and UKRI's R4RI already allow it. Stroke funders can require it and budget for deposit.

5

Institutions · costs nothing

Open-science items in promotion criteria

Zero of 92 guidelines do today. Utrecht dropped the impact factor in 2022; Columbia and Calgary count datasets.

6

Trialists · needs infrastructure

Embargoed sharing as the default

Twelve months after the primary publication, already written into COMPASS and ACT-GLOBAL. VISTA as the trusted archive; federated or synthetic access where consent or GDPR blocks sharing.

Items 1, 3 and 5 cost nothing. Items 2, 4 and 6 need money or infrastructure that mostly already exists.

Two and a half minutes. Read each title, then one sentence. Say which items cost nothing and which need money. Invite the WSO leadership in the room to take item 3 home.
