03  Where the fields next door are now

The rules now exist. Practice is catching up.

What they agreed on

2013

Registered Reports launch at Cortex, a neuroscience and neuropsychology journal; more than 300 journals offer them now

2015

TOP guidelines for journals, 5,000+ signatories; revised as TOP 2025, which certifies, not just asks

2016

BIDS, one standard for organising and sharing neuroimaging data

2017

A manifesto for reproducible science; COBIDAS, the reporting standard for MRI studies

2024

Psychological Science makes open data and materials the default and checks that results reproduce

2025

PECANS, a Delphi consensus checklist for cognitive and neuropsychological studies, 206 experts

Sixty seconds. Psychology did not fix itself: it measured itself, agreed on rules, and is still short of them. The newest and largest test, SCORE (Nature, April 2026): 151 of 274 claims (55%) from 164 papers in 54 journals replicated, median effect r = 0.25 down to 0.10, and authors made data available for only 24% of 600 papers (Miske 2026). Many Labs 2: 54% of 28 findings. In 2022 only 7% of psychology articles were preregistered and 14% shared raw data (Hardwicke 2024); the authors' words: transparency 'continues to be widely neglected'. Registered Reports work where they are used: 44% positive results vs 96% (Scheel 2021), rated higher on rigour and no lower on novelty (Soderberg 2021). The best-known evidence that rigour alone fixes replication, Protzko 2023 (86%), was retracted in September 2024 because its preregistration claims were false. Neuroimaging: 70 teams analysed one fMRI dataset and no two pipelines matched (NARPS, Nature 2020); cluster inference gave up to 70% false positives (Eklund 2016); brain-wide associations need thousands of participants (Marek 2022), still debated. Neuropsychology: only 1 of 62 Cortex Registered Reports used patients; about half of 47 neuropsychologists named field-specific barriers, mostly sensitive clinical data (Binney 2025); PECANS (2025) is the field's own consensus. Closest to this room: lesion network mapping, used in more than 200 studies, was challenged in Nature Neuroscience in January 2026 with public data only (van den Heuvel 2026); the replies are in the same journal. That debate is possible because the data were open. Lesion mapping itself: maps shift by about 16 mm with vascular anatomy (Mah 2014), and fewer than 40% of N = 30 resamples reach significance (Lorca-Puls 2018). Sources: talk/evidence_intro.md.
