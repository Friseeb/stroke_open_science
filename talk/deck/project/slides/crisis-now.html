Sixty seconds. Psychology did not fix itself: it measured itself, agreed on rules, and is still short of them. Agreed: Registered Reports (launched at Cortex in 2013, now more than 300 journals); the TOP guidelines (2015, 5,000+ signatories, revised as TOP 2025, which adds independent verification to disclosing and sharing); PECANS (2025), a Delphi consensus checklist for cognitive and neuropsychological studies, 206 experts. Now: the SCORE project (Nature, April 2026) replicated 151 of 274 claims from social and behavioural science papers published 2009-2018 (55%), effects fell from r = 0.25 to 0.10, and authors made data available for only 24% of 600 papers (Miske 2026). In 2022, 7% of psychology papers were preregistered and 14% shared raw data (Hardwicke 2024). Only 1 of 62 Registered Reports in Cortex studied patients; the most common barrier neuropsychologists name is sharing sensitive clinical data (12 of 23; Binney 2025). Registered Reports report 44% positive results against 96% (Scheel 2021). The best-known claim that rigour alone restores replication (Protzko 2023) was retracted in 2024. Closest to this room: lesion network mapping, used in 200+ studies, was challenged in Nature Neuroscience in January 2026 using only public data; that debate exists because the data were open.
