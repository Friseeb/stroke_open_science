Published open = gold, diamond or hybrid in OpenAlex · free to read = any status other than closed

The good-news slide, on the strict measure. Published open means gold, diamond or hybrid in OpenAlex: an open-access journal or an open licence, fixed at publication. In stroke it more than doubled: 29% in 2015, 47% in 2020, 64% in 2024, 67% in 2025. Stroke has led the four fields every year since 2020, by 4 to 6 points since 2022; in 2025 cardiology 62%, neurology 61%, neuroscience 59%. Over 2015-2025: stroke 50%, neuroscience 49%, cardiology 48%, neurology 45%. Let it land, then turn it on the next one. If asked why not the bigger number: free to read by any route adds bronze (free on the publisher site without a licence, which the publisher can withdraw) and green (a repository copy); it is 75% for stroke in 2025, but it depends on the database. On the same articles PubMed's own free-full-text flag gives 68% for 2022 against OpenAlex's 74%, and the gap is mostly bronze and green; for gold, diamond and hybrid the two agree on 91 to 100% of articles (sample of 8,000 articles, talk/outputs/tables/oa_openalex_vs_pubmed_sample.csv). Cardiology leads on free to read (78% vs 75% in 2024) mostly through bronze (15% of its articles, stroke 7%), including free conference abstracts in JACC and European Heart Journal supplements; on published open it trails stroke. The dashboard's open-access indicator is the free-to-read one, so the dashboard link shows 75%. 2025 needs no caveat on this measure: the route is fixed at publication, and only green and bronze still fill in. Do not rank neuroscience: about 45% of it comes only from the title term "neural", much of it machine learning; without those papers it is 63% published open in 2025, still below stroke.
