Rules produced statements; incentives produce data. Full rows: data obtainable from surgical trials before and after the ICMJE statement requirement, 3.1% and 3.1% (Bergeat 2022, JAMA Netw Open); open-data badges in the only medical randomised trial, at BMJ Open, OR 0.9, while the same badges took Psychological Science from under 3% to 39% open data (Rowhani-Farid 2020; Kidwell 2016); 0 of 92 biomedical promotion guidelines worldwide mention data sharing, registration or open access (Rice 2020, BMJ); positive results 44% in Registered Reports vs 96% in the standard literature, so changing when review happens changes what gets published (Scheel 2021). Original line: stroke researchers are not less honest than psychologists; they respond to the same incentives, and ours point the other way. Cardiology survey, n = 198: funding named the most essential incentive; "policy alone will not lead to implementation" (Cobey 2024). The pivot of the talk. Four numbers, then the sentence in serif. No more studies here; the evidence brief has them if asked. Journals, checked October 2026: none of 14 stroke and neurology journals offers Registered Reports (Stroke, IJS, ESJ, JSCVD, Stroke and Vascular Neurology, Journal of Stroke, Cerebrovascular Diseases, Translational Stroke Research, JCBFM, Neurology, JAMA Neurology, Lancet Neurology, Annals of Neurology, Frontiers in Neurology), and none is on the Center for Open Science list of about 300 journals that do. Frontiers in Neuroscience offers them; Frontiers in Neurology, same publisher, does not. A few take protocols for non-trial designs (Stroke and Vascular Neurology, Cerebrovascular Diseases for funded studies, Lancet Neurology, JAMA Neurology, Frontiers in Neurology for a fee), but none gives in-principle acceptance. JSCVD's instructions: 'Purely observational studies… do not require registration.' ICMJE encourages registration of non-trial designs but does not require it. The same split across biomedicine: a registered protocol in 67% of clinical trials against 3% of other research (Serghiou 2021, PLoS Biol); 10% of observational studies in the top five general journals were registered in 2022 (Leducq 2024). Preclinical: about 550 preregistered animal studies worldwide by 2025, and 10% of Swiss animal study directors have ever preregistered (Priboi 2026).
