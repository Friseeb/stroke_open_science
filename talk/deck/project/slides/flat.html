Abstracts and metadata, 2015 to 2024: lower bounds

Card details: data in a repository 0.2% of stroke articles in 2024, 0.2% in 2015, never above 0.6%; code link under 0.1% every year 2015-2024, GitHub in 2.5% of 2024 full texts; preprint version 2.7% in 2024, 1.5% over 2015-2025, a lower bound and partly publisher-driven; registration ID in the abstract: 1.5% of observational, imaging, lab and animal studies in 2024 (0.8% in 2015), against 35% of trials (21% in 2015) and 22% of systematic reviews (PROSPERO); pooled, 5.3% in 2024 from 2.8% in 2015. High impact is barely better at sharing: top-cited or IF 10+ articles (16% of the corpus) are no more often published open (49% vs 51%), though more are free to read (74% vs 65%, through publisher free access and repository copies: bronze 14% vs 9%, green 12% vs 5%); registration 10.0% vs 3.5%, but data 0.45% vs 0.34%, code 0.08% vs 0.06%. Say the caveat before anyone says it for you: lower bounds from abstracts. Preprints did move, from under 0.2% before 2019 to 2.7% in 2024, but only as far as OpenAlex links them (it merged many preprint records into their articles in early October), and about half are on Research Square or SSRN, mostly publisher routes at submission (In Review, Lancet and Elsevier). GitHub in full text: 2.5% of the 1,500 audited 2024 articles, 1.3% pooled over the decade with every year weighted the same; the code chart counts GitHub or code links in abstracts, and the high-impact line also counts Zenodo and OSF links. Registration is a trial habit, not a field habit (the dashboard's new Design filter splits every indicator into trials and other studies). Read in the full text (open-access reports, 2022-2025), 68% of stroke trials state their own registration, 45% of systematic reviews and 7% of everything else; in abstracts, 35%, 22% and 1.5% (2024). So most trials are registered, though a third of open-access trial reports still state no registration, and the rest of stroke research almost never is: the cohorts, registries, imaging, lab and animal studies that make up 88% of what we publish. Of registered stroke trials, 57% name ClinicalTrials.gov and 16% ChiCTR; the rest ISRCTN, ANZCTR, UMIN, KCT, IRCT and others. The pooled rise, 2.8% to 5.3%, comes mostly from trials and systematic reviews. Same picture next door: trials 23-31% in abstracts and other papers 0.5-2.4% in neurology, neuroscience and cardiology. And across biomedicine: a registered protocol in 67% of clinical trials against 3% of other research (Serghiou 2021, PLoS Biol). Method: a paper is a trial when PubMed indexes it with a trial publication type or its title and abstract describe one; 6.8% of stroke articles. Sources: talk/outputs/tables/stroke_registration_by_design.csv. The neuroscience preprint line also reflects "neural": machine-learning papers, many on arXiv. Then the pivot: so we read the full text.
