High impact is barely better at sharing: top-cited or IF 10+ articles (16% of the corpus) are 74% open access vs 65%, registration 10.0% vs 3.5%, but data 0.45% vs 0.34%, code 0.08% vs 0.06%. These are lower bounds from abstracts and metadata. So we read the full text.

Say the caveat before anyone says it for you: lower bounds from abstracts. Preprints did move, from under 0.2% before 2019 to 2.7% in 2024, but only as far as OpenAlex links them (it merged many preprint records into their articles in early October), and about half are on Research Square or SSRN, mostly publisher routes at submission (In Review, Lancet and Elsevier). GitHub in full text: 2.5% of the 1,500 audited 2024 articles, 1.3% pooled over the decade with every year weighted the same; the code chart counts GitHub or code links in abstracts, and the high-impact line also counts Zenodo and OSF links. Registration nearly doubled, 2.8% to 5.3%, from a low base: stroke 4.6% over the decade, cardiology 4.1%, neurology 2.4%, neuroscience 1.0%; neuroscience's term "neural" pulls in machine-learning papers, and without them it is still only about 1.5%.
