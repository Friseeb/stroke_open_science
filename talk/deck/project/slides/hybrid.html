The benchmark compares stroke with the same three fields

Fifty seconds, first person. As a stroke neurologist, I went to cognitive neuroscience: lesions are how we learn where functions live and how brain networks connect. Stroke is hybrid by definition, and we work with all three of these fields every day. Neuroscience gives us the lesion: lesion-symptom mapping, disconnection, connectomes. Neurology gives us the trials, the outcomes and recovery. Cardiology gives us the heart and the vessels: atrial fibrillation, PFO, vascular risk. So stroke inherits its neighbours' methods, and also their problems, which is the next part; and these are the three fields the benchmark compares stroke with. The colours are the same in every chart that follows: stroke red, neuroscience green, neurology blue, cardiology yellow; the circles sit in the legend's order (neuroscience top left, neurology top right, cardiology below). On the neuroscience side, a lesion study is built from open parts at every step: segmentation (MRIcron, ITK-SNAP, LINDA, nnU-Net; DeepISLES, an ensemble of the ISLES 2022 winners, was validated on 1,685 external scans and neuroradiologists preferred its masks to manual ones in a Turing-like test), normalisation that respects the lesion (cost-function masking, enantiomorphic normalisation, the SPM Clinical Toolbox, ANTs), lesion-symptom mapping (VLSM since Bates 2003; NiiStat, SVR-LSM, LESYMAP), disconnection (lesion network mapping, BCBtoolkit, the Lesion Quantification Toolkit) and sharing (BIDS, OpenNeuro with 1,913 public datasets today, NeuroVault). Most are BSD, GPL or Apache licensed; a few need MATLAB. The datasets are real: ATLAS v2.0 (1,271 MRIs, 955 public; raw data under a data use agreement), ISLES 2022 (250 of 400 public), the Aphasia Recovery Cohort (230 people with aphasia, 902 sessions, CC0) and SOOP (1,715 admissions with clinical MRI, CC0) on OpenNeuro. Open data did not bring open code by itself: 12 of 17 methods published on ATLAS released no code (Liew 2022). The contrast that started this project: on the neuroscience side the tools and data are open; on the clinical side, trial data and code are not.
