Forty-five seconds. Audits of open practices exist for neurology, cardiology and general medicine (Rauh 2020; Heckerman 2025; Scutt 2020); none existed for stroke. What it measures, in detail: open access and its route (diamond, gold, hybrid, green, bronze); data and code (repository links, GitHub, data-availability statements); preprints (a version on a preprint server); registration (trial registries, PROSPERO, Registered Reports). Abstract and metadata signals are lower bounds; the full-text audit narrows the gap for data sharing. You cannot improve what you do not measure; this is implementation science applied to research practice itself. Audits exist next door, none for stroke, so we built one that keeps running. One published run, 13 October 2026: 231,580 OpenAlex records from six title terms, 2015 to 2026, of which 140,281 are stroke journal articles and reviews with a DOI from 2015 to 2025. The full-text audit reads up to 1,500 open-access articles per year in each field through Europe PMC, 16,362 for stroke. Open-access full text only, so those rates run high, and a pooled rate gives every year the same weight, so I lead with single years and say "over the decade" when I pool. Everything that follows is on the dashboard, where anyone can change the selection. Neuroscience's title term "neural" brings in about 45% of that corpus and also catches machine-learning papers. If asked: neurology 129,644, cardiology 312,703, neuroscience 509,973 articles.
