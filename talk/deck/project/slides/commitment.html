Recomputed every six months. Say the n out loud: Charité 129 audited articles, Oxford 177, Hamburg-Eppendorf 109; Nature Communications 30, PeerJ 31 (25 of them deposit). The top two in the figure are both Charité: the Center for Stroke Research Berlin (5.2×) is part of it (4.4×). The figure ranks the 60 institutions with the most audited articles (82 or more); with every institution at 50 or more, the German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases (5.6×, 61 audited) and the Berlin Institute of Health at Charité (5.2×, 64) also lead, then Zurich (4.7×, 64). Small counts are a hint, not a ranking: GitHub is rare (1.3%), so one more GitHub mention in 100 audited articles adds about 0.26, and the order shifts between runs. Countries need 50 audited articles: at 30, Russia, with 43 audited, would be second at 2.2×. The baselines pool the 2015-2025 audit with every year weighted the same, and the signals rose over the decade (deposit or public source 0.7% in 2015, 9.8% in 2025), so an index also reflects which years an entity published in. Stroke sits above the average, not among the leaders of the 99 journals; the dashboard keeps only the 40 largest journals and 25 largest countries, so there Stroke ranks third and Belgium and Ireland do not appear. Oxford is also listed as a funder in OpenAlex (4.9×, 33 audited); it is an institution, so I leave it out of the funders. Also on the lists: PeerJ deposits in 81%; funders NIDCD, NIBIB, HHS, Horizon 2020 and BMBF; Ireland 1.7×. The point is not the names: the measurement exists, is cheap, and is what a prize would recognise.
