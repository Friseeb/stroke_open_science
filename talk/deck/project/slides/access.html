Forty-five seconds. Access has two halves. Off the slide, say: citations did not change in the randomised trial; in our data the 1.6x is 1.3x once funding is matched, and deposited data earn no citation advantage. Readers: in the only randomised trials of open access (Davis, BMJ 2008; FASEB J 2011), open articles got 89% more full-text downloads and no more citations at one and three years. In our own stroke corpus, articles free to read by any route are cited about 1.6 times as often as closed ones in the same journal and year, and about 1.3 times once funding status is matched (funded papers are 41% of open and 20% of closed articles); data deposited in a repository earn no citation advantage at all. So: open work is read more, cited somewhat more, and sharing data is not rewarded by citations; that is why the proposals at the end matter. Tools: thrombectomy reaches a median 2.8% of eligible patients across countries, 88% lower in low-income countries (MT-GLASS). Commercial imaging AI helps where hospitals can pay for it: a cluster randomised trial of Viz.ai cut door-to-groin by 11 minutes, and thrombectomy rates rose in English hospitals using Brainomix. But licences cost about 15,000 to 30,000 pounds a year per centre (NICE, manufacturers' estimates); in Korea these tools are not covered by national insurance, and the price of non-contrast CT LVO screening is capped at 18,100 won per scan. The late-window selection rules came from one vendor's software (DAWN, DEFUSE 3), and on the same 54 patients two packages disagreed on thrombectomy eligibility in 15% (Mallon 2022), although newer packages mostly agree. Simpler imaging works in the late window (MR CLEAN-LATE; CLEAR). The open alternative exists for MRI: DeepISLES, three challenge winners merged, Apache licence, validated on 1,685 Johns Hopkins scans, preferred to manual tracing by neuroradiologists. The gap is stewardship: no open stroke tool is regulated, maintained, or tested in a low- or middle-income country, and none for CT perfusion or LVO detection has peer-reviewed validation. If asked about Korea: 22.5% of ischaemic stroke patients live in a district without a thrombectomy centre (2021).
