02  Why open science

The fields next door had their crisis first

Psychology

36%

of 100 published findings replicated with a significant result; effect sizes about half the originals. Open Science Collaboration, Science 2015

Neuroscience

21%

median statistical power of neuroscience studies. Button et al., Nature Reviews Neuroscience 2013

Preclinical cancer

6 of 53

landmark findings that Amgen could reproduce. Begley and Ellis, Nature 2012

Across science

70%

of 1,576 researchers had failed to reproduce another group’s experiment; 52% called it a significant crisis. Baker, Nature 2016

Why it happened

Small samples, flexible analyses, selective reporting. Four routine analysis choices raise a 5% false-positive rate to 61% (Simmons 2011); 63% of 2,155 psychologists admitted not reporting all their measures (John 2012).

Psychology and neuroscience measured themselves, it hurt, and they changed how they publish.

Forty-five seconds. These are the speaker’s former fields; say so. Four numbers, then why: small samples, flexible analyses, selective reporting. The next slide says what those fields agreed and where they are now.
