van den Heuvel 2026 · Ingall 2004 · Nissen 2007 · Sena 2010

Forty-five seconds. One case from each neighbour, then our own; the bottom line of each card is what went wrong. Neuroscience, in my own method: lesion network mapping has been used in more than 200 studies. In January 2026 a Nature Neuroscience re-analysis (van den Heuvel and colleagues), using only public data (the GSP1000 connectome, NeuroVault maps, LesionBank), argued that it maps any set of lesions, even random ones, onto the same normative network. Present it as a live debate: Zalesky and Cash wrote on null models (Nat Neurosci, March 2026); Pini, Salvalaggio and Corbetta on its biological limitations (Nat Neurosci, May 2026); Siddiqi, Fox and colleagues (preprint) report that specificity holds across 1,090 lesions from 34 studies; a multicentre study of 2,950 stroke patients (Petersen, medRxiv) found domain-specific maps with case-control designs, so specificity depends on design. The point for this talk: the challenge and the replies were possible because the maps and the connectome were public. Michael Fox: 'Such debate will move the field forward.' Neurology: the NINDS rt-PA data were closed for eight years; when an independent committee re-analysed them, the benefit held. That is the next slide. Cardiology: rosiglitazone. Nissen and Wolski used the trial results GSK had to post under a 2004 New York settlement: 42 trials, odds ratio for myocardial infarction 1.43 (1.03-1.98) (NEJM 2007). Regulators restricted the drug in 2010; the FDA lifted the restrictions in 2013 after the RECORD readjudication. When a Yale group obtained individual patient data for 33 trials, 26 of 29 showed more MIs than the summaries earlier meta-analyses had used (Wallach, BMJ 2020). Preclinical stroke: only 10 of 525 animal stroke publications (2%) reported no significant effect on infarct volume; publication bias accounts for about a third of reported efficacy (31.3% falling to 23.8% after trim-and-fill) and about 14% of experiments were never published (Sena 2010, CAMARADES). Behind it: 1,026 experimental treatments for acute stroke, and the 114 that reached clinical use were no more effective in animals than the 912 tested only in animals (O'Collins 2006); NXY-059 looked effective in animals, but only 2 of 9 publications reported randomisation, concealed allocation and blinded outcome assessment (Macleod 2008), and SAINT I was positive while SAINT II was neutral. If asked about lesion mapping more broadly: fewer than 40% of samples of 30 patients reached significance, against virtually all at 180 or more (Lorca-Puls 2018); the vascular shape of stroke lesions displaces lesion-symptom maps by about 16 mm (Mah 2014; Sperber and Karnath 2017); shared resting-state data showed cluster false-positive rates of up to 70% against a nominal 5% in standard fMRI software (Eklund 2016). Keep the constructs separate: computational reproducibility reruns the same data and code; replicability tests a finding in new data; power describes study sensitivity (National Academies 2019, doi:10.17226/25303). If asked about psychology, where the crisis was named: 36% of 100 findings replicated (Open Science Collaboration 2015); ten years on, 55% of 274 claims (SCORE, Nature, April 2026), with data available for only 24% of 600 papers; in 2022, 7% of psychology articles were preregistered and 14% shared raw data (Hardwicke 2024); 70% of 1,576 researchers had failed to reproduce another group's experiment (Baker 2016). If asked about cancer: Amgen confirmed 6 of 53 landmark preclinical findings (Begley 2012). Sources: talk/evidence_reanalysis.md; talk/evidence_intro.md.
