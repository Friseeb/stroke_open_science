Yet 12 of 17 methods built on ATLAS reported no public code.

Fifty seconds, first person. I came to stroke from imaging and neuroscience. There, a lesion study is built from open parts at every step: segmentation (ITK-SNAP; DeepISLES, from the ISLES 2022 winners), normalisation that respects the lesion (SPM Clinical Toolbox, ANTs), lesion-symptom mapping (VLSM since 2003, LESYMAP), network and disconnection mapping (BCBtoolkit, lesion network mapping) and sharing (BIDS, OpenNeuro). The data are real: ATLAS v2.0 has 1,271 traced MRIs, 955 public; ISLES 2022 released 250 of 400 cases; the Aphasia Recovery Cohort is CC0 on OpenNeuro. The red line is the bridge: open data alone did not bring open code (Liew 2022). Then the turn: when I moved to clinical stroke, the trial data and code were not there.
